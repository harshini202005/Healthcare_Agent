# Healthcare FAQ — Common Health Questions

## Blood Pressure

Normal blood pressure is below 120/80 mmHg. Elevated is 120–129 systolic with less than 80 diastolic. Stage 1 hypertension is 130–139/80–89 mmHg. Stage 2 is 140/90 mmHg or higher. A hypertensive crisis (above 180/120) requires immediate medical attention.

To manage blood pressure: reduce sodium intake (under 2,300 mg/day), exercise regularly (150 min/week of moderate activity), maintain healthy weight, limit alcohol, avoid smoking, manage stress, and take prescribed medications consistently.

## Diabetes

Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune condition where the pancreas produces little or no insulin. It requires insulin therapy. Type 2 diabetes occurs when the body doesn't use insulin well and is often linked to lifestyle factors. Type 2 is managed with diet, exercise, oral medications, and sometimes insulin.

Normal fasting blood glucose is under 100 mg/dL. Prediabetes is 100–125 mg/dL. Diabetes is diagnosed at 126 mg/dL or higher on two separate tests.

Symptoms of diabetes include increased thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, and slow-healing wounds.

## Heart Health

Chest pain that is severe, crushing, or accompanied by shortness of breath, sweating, or pain radiating to the left arm or jaw requires immediate emergency medical care — call emergency services (911/112).

Risk factors for heart disease: high blood pressure, high cholesterol, smoking, diabetes, obesity, family history, physical inactivity, and poor diet.

To protect heart health: eat a diet low in saturated fat and sodium, exercise regularly, avoid smoking, limit alcohol, manage stress, and attend regular checkups.

## Cholesterol

Total cholesterol should ideally be below 200 mg/dL. LDL ("bad") cholesterol should be under 100 mg/dL for most people, and under 70 mg/dL for high-risk individuals. HDL ("good") cholesterol should be above 40 mg/dL for men and 50 mg/dL for women.

Diet changes to improve cholesterol: reduce saturated fats (red meat, full-fat dairy), eliminate trans fats, increase soluble fiber (oats, beans, fruits), add omega-3 fatty acids (fish, flaxseed), and exercise regularly.

## Sleep and Insomnia

Adults need 7–9 hours of quality sleep per night. Teens need 8–10 hours. Chronic sleep deprivation increases risk of obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and mental health issues.

Good sleep hygiene: maintain a consistent sleep schedule, keep the bedroom cool, dark, and quiet, avoid screens 1 hour before bed, limit caffeine after 2 PM, avoid large meals and alcohol close to bedtime, and get regular exercise (but not within 2–3 hours of sleep).

Insomnia affecting daily function for more than 3 months should be evaluated by a doctor. Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is the first-line treatment.

## Anxiety and Mental Health

Anxiety disorders are the most common mental health conditions. Symptoms include persistent worry, restlessness, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, and sleep problems.

Evidence-based treatments: cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), mindfulness-based therapy, medication (SSRIs, SNRIs as prescribed), regular physical exercise, stress reduction techniques, and social support.

Seek medical help if anxiety significantly impacts daily life, work, or relationships, or if you experience panic attacks.

## Common Cold vs. Flu

Common cold: gradual onset, mild fever or none, runny/stuffy nose prominent, mild fatigue, sneezing common. Usually resolves in 7–10 days.

Flu: rapid onset (within hours), high fever (38–40°C), severe body aches, extreme fatigue, headache, dry cough. Can last 1–2 weeks. Complications (pneumonia) can be serious in elderly, very young, or immunocompromised.

Antibiotics do NOT treat colds or flu (they are viral). Rest, fluids, and over-the-counter symptom relief are standard treatment. Annual flu vaccination is recommended for everyone over 6 months.

## Back Pain

Most back pain (85–90% of cases) is non-specific and resolves within 4–6 weeks with conservative treatment: staying active, applying heat/ice, over-the-counter pain relievers (ibuprofen, paracetamol), and gentle stretching.

See a doctor promptly for back pain accompanied by: bowel or bladder problems, numbness/weakness in legs, fever, unexplained weight loss, or pain following trauma.

Avoid prolonged bed rest. Gentle movement and staying active are more effective than rest for most back pain.

## Headaches and Migraines

Tension headaches: dull, pressure-like pain around the head, often triggered by stress, poor posture, dehydration, or eyestrain.

Migraines: moderate-to-severe throbbing pain, usually one-sided, often with nausea, vomiting, and sensitivity to light and sound. Can last 4–72 hours. Triggers include hormonal changes, certain foods, stress, sleep changes, and sensory stimuli.

Seek emergency care for: sudden severe headache ("thunderclap"), headache with fever and stiff neck, headache after head trauma, or headache with vision changes, confusion, or weakness.

## Nutrition and Diet Basics

A balanced diet includes: fruits and vegetables (at least 5 portions/day), whole grains, lean proteins (legumes, fish, poultry, eggs), healthy fats (olive oil, avocado, nuts), and adequate hydration (6–8 glasses of water/day).

Limit: ultra-processed foods, added sugars (under 25g/day for women, 36g/day for men), saturated fats, and sodium (under 2,300 mg/day).

Body Mass Index (BMI): Underweight < 18.5, Normal 18.5–24.9, Overweight 25–29.9, Obese ≥ 30.

## Exercise Guidelines

Adults should aim for: 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week (brisk walking, cycling) OR 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity (running, fast cycling), PLUS muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days per week.

Regular exercise reduces risk of: cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, certain cancers, depression, anxiety, and dementia. It also improves sleep quality, bone health, and overall wellbeing.

Even short bouts of activity (10-minute walks) count toward the weekly goal.

## Vaccinations for Adults

Recommended adult vaccinations: annual flu shot, COVID-19 (as per current guidelines), Td/Tdap booster every 10 years, Shingrix (shingles) for adults 50+, Pneumococcal vaccine for adults 65+, HPV vaccine through age 26 (and some 27–45 year olds), Hepatitis B if not previously vaccinated.

Vaccination schedules vary by age, health status, occupation, and travel history. Consult your GP for a personalized vaccination plan.
